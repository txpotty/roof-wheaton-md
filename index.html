Common Roofing Problems

Roofs, regardless of their material or construction, are susceptible to various issues over time. Identifying these problems early can save you significantly on potential future repairs. Here are some common roofing problems we address at Wheaton Roofing Pros:

  • Missing or Damaged Shingles: Exposed areas can lead to water damage and further deterioration of the roof.
  • Roof Leaks: Water intrusion can damage ceilings, walls, and insulation, fostering mold growth.
  • Buckling or Curling Shingles: Indicates aging or defective shingles, reducing their protective capabilities.
  • Granule Loss: The protective granules on shingles gradually wear off, exposing the asphalt to the elements.
  • Damaged Flashing: Compromised flashing around chimneys, vents, and skylights can allow water to seep in.
  • Sagging Roof Deck: Suggests structural weakness and potential collapse, often due to water damage or age.
  • Moss or Algae Growth: Can trap moisture, leading to rot and deterioration of roofing materials.
  • Clogged Gutters: Obstructed gutters can cause water to back up and damage the roof and fascia boards.

    How often should I have my roof inspected?

    It’s recommended to have your roof inspected at least once a year, preferably in the spring and fall. Regular inspections can identify potential problems early, preventing costly repairs down the road. Additionally, after severe weather events like hailstorms or hurricanes, it’s wise to schedule an inspection to assess any damage.

    What are the signs that I need a new roof?

    Several signs indicate that you may need a new roof. Look for excessive granule loss, curling or buckling shingles, frequent leaks despite repairs, a sagging roof deck, and significant moss or algae growth. If your roof is over 20 years old, it may also be time to consider a replacement, even if there are no visible signs of damage.

    How long does a roof replacement take?

    The duration of a roof replacement depends on several factors, including the size and complexity of the roof, the type of roofing material used, and weather conditions. Generally, a typical roof replacement can take anywhere from a few days to a week to complete.
